Quarterstone

A two-player online co-op action RPG seen at 45°. The world is a 1-metre grid carved from 25-centimetre voxels — every canyon wall, riverbed and arch is made of the same cubes, so terrain can be authored, deformed and streamed as one honest data structure. Grass and water are the exception: never voxelised, always moving.

Terrain vocabulary

Five shapes the generator is allowed to speak in

Every feature is cut from the same voxel field, so they compose: a river can run through a canyon, an arch can span either. Ranges below are the authoring limits the generator samples between.

FeatureMetricsBehaviour

Canyon

3–5 m wide · 3–4 m deep

Cuts the mesa into two playfields. Walls expose banded strata one voxel at a time; ledges are legal footing, so verticality is combat space, not scenery.

River

1–4 m wide · 0.3 m bed

Meanders on its own noise. Narrow enough to leap at 1 m, wide enough to need a log or arch at 4 m. Surface is a shader, not voxels — it drifts with the wind direction.

Hills

1–3 m rise

The default ground state. Slopes over 45° auto-switch from grass to rock so the palette reads the traversal rule before the player tests it.

Arc

4–6 m span · 3–5 m rise

A stone span left standing where the field eroded. Doubles as a bridge and a sniping perch; the underside is a covered fight.

Props

0.5–4 m

Trees, boulders, fallen logs — all voxelised on the same 25 cm lattice, so they chip, topple and bridge using the terrain's own rules.

Grass

0.3–0.6 m blades

Deliberately not voxels. Two sine bands of wind plus a trample radius around every character; the softness is what keeps the cubes from reading as a spreadsheet.

Gear system

Frames and sockets

A weapon is not a stat block — it is a frame with base stats and a socket count. Tech and magic upgrades drop into the sockets, and adjacent upgrades read each other: a pairing produces a third behaviour neither has alone.

Frames never level up. They gain reach, weight and socket count — everything else is what you bolt in.

Tech upgrades — machined, finite, precise Arc capacitor Recoil damper Servo edge Kinetic battery Range finder
Magic upgrades — hungry, reactive, cheap to feed Frostbind Emberweft Sap-siphon Stoneblood Echo rune

Arc capacitor + Frostbind

Chain-frost

The capacitor's discharge carries the bind along wet ground and standing water.

3 targets · 18% slow · 2.5 s

Servo edge + Sap-siphon

Bleed engine

Faster swings feed the siphon; the siphon pays the servo's heat back as stamina.

+22% swing · 1.4 hp/hit

Stoneblood + Emberweft

Slagform

Two magics stack instead of trading — armour gains mass and a contact burn, and loses dodge.

+40 armour · −15% dodge

Adjacency

Only upgrades in neighbouring sockets combine, so socket order is a real decision, not an inventory formality.

Cross-discipline

Tech + magic pairs bend behaviour; same-discipline pairs amplify a stat and take a drawback.

Co-op reading

A socketed pair is visible on the weapon at iso distance — colour and silhouette — so your partner can read your build across the field.

Status: concept only. No game code has been written yet — this plate exists to lock art direction, terrain metrics and the gear system before the engine, netcode and Steam integration (appid 480, Spacewar, for development) go in.
