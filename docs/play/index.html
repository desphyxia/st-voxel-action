Quarterstone · playable build

The first thing here you can actually play. One 64 × 64 m window of a seeded world, one character, and the movement budget the terrain was sized against. No combat, no second player, no enemies — this exists to answer one question, which is whether moving through this world feels like anything.

WASD to move, camera-relative · Space to jump · Q/E to turn the view · R to respawn · mouse or right stick to aim. Click the view first to give it the keyboard.

The budget, and nothing else

Only run speed was chosen. Gravity, jump speed and airtime are solved from these numbers so that a jump clears exactly the gap above and no further — which is why a 3–5 m canyon is still an obstacle. Change the budget in src/gen/constants.mjs and the character and the terrain move together.

Controls

Remappable from the first commit, because retrofitting it is the expensive version. Bindings are physical key codes, so WASD stays under the same fingers on any layout. A gamepad works too: left stick moves, right stick aims, A jumps, the shoulders turn the view.

What this is not

The instanced-box renderer is the concept plate's, not the shipping approach — the greedy mesher replaces it once streaming forces the issue. There is no second player, no netcode and no combat. The world is one window: walk to the edge and you meet a wall, because that is where the generated window stops.

Aim drives facing and nothing else yet. What a mouse point and a stick direction each mean once there is an ability to place is still open.

Quarterstone · isometric voxel co-op · the terrain generator lives in src/gen, collision and the controller in src/sim, and both are inlined into this page by tools/bundle-gen.mjs.
