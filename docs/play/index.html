Quarterstone · playable build

The first thing here you can actually play. One 64 × 64 m window of a seeded world, the movement budget the terrain was sized against, one committed swing, three sentry automata still holding a position, caches out in the world with modules and fusion recipes in them, and — if you open a second window — someone else in it with you. This is move, fight and fuse.

Quarterstone is played in landscape.

WASD to move, camera-relative · Space to jump · click or F to swing · Shift to dodge · Q/E to turn the view · R to respawn · mouse or right stick to aim. Walk over a cache or a fallen machine to take what is in it, and seat it in the lattice below. Fullscreen fills the screen; Esc leaves it. On a phone, hold it sideways: your left thumb is a stick wherever you put it, and the buttons on the right cut, dodge and jump. Click the view first to give it the keyboard.

Playing alone. Host a game opens a second window on this machine and puts another character in the same world — the host decides what happened, the guest predicts and reconciles.

The budget, and nothing else

Only run speed was chosen. Gravity, jump speed and airtime are solved from these numbers so that a jump clears exactly the gap above and no further — which is why a 3–5 m canyon is still an obstacle. Change the budget in src/gen/constants.mjs and the character and the terrain move together.

Controls

Remappable from the first commit, because retrofitting it is the expensive version. Bindings are physical key codes, so WASD stays under the same fingers on any layout. A gamepad works too: left stick moves, right stick aims, A jumps, the shoulders turn the view.

On a touch screen the same table drives on-screen controls: your left thumb is a stick wherever in the left half you put it — push it half way and you walk half speed — and the buttons on the right cut, dodge, jump and turn the view. A tap on open ground deliberately does nothing: the mouse binding for a swing would otherwise fire on every touch. Landscape only, because this camera is framed so a 5 m canyon and its far rim both fit.

The lattice

Pick a module, then a socket.

Four sockets, five shared edges. A module seated in one does its own small thing; two from different traditions across a shared edge can fuse into something neither does alone — which is the premise, since tech, magic and biological were rival schools that never combined. Two of a kind side by side is just two modules.

You do not start knowing how. Fusion recipes are learned from the world, out of the caches at the landmark and the routed sites — which is the first reason this world has anywhere worth walking to. The machines only ever drop tech, because a war machine drops its own discipline, so fighting alone can never give you two traditions to fuse.

One swing

Wind-up, active, recovery. During the active window you go nowhere at all — committed means committed, and you cannot jump, vault or dodge out of it. A dodge can cancel the recovery, which is the only escape hatch and costs stamina you might have wanted for the next swing.

None of these numbers are balance. Issue #9 decides what a hit is worth; what this is for is the question underneath it — whether a committed swing reads at 45°, where the character is forty pixels tall and half the arc is foreshortened. That is why the arc is drawn on the ground: the blade itself is three voxels wide and mostly behind the character.

The posts are there to practise on. The machines are not.

The sentry automaton

A tech war machine still holding a position against an enemy that left. Squat and wide, because from 45° you mostly see the top of things — so the readable surface is its top plate, and that is where the tell goes.

It stops dead, rises, and the plate flares. The stopping is the tell that works from any distance: everything else in a fight is moving, so a machine that suddenly is not reads instantly. The rise changes its footprint, which is the one silhouette change an overhead view can actually see. The wedge on the ground says where.

Its recovery is longer than your whole swing, so dodging through the strike buys a free hit rather than just survival. It outranges you, so you have to come inside its reach to use that. It does not vault — a heavy machine goes around, and going around properly is the navigation graph in #15.

What this is not

The instanced-box renderer is the concept plate's, not the shipping approach — the greedy mesher replaces it once streaming forces the issue. One frame of eight, nine modules and six fusions of however many there will be, one enemy archetype of the twelve #6 will specify, no respawning and no stash. The world is one window: walk to the edge and you meet a wall, because that is where the generated window stops.

The two windows talk through postMessage, which is a prototype's transport and nothing more. It sits behind the same three methods Steam Networking will implement, so none of the session logic knows the difference — which was the point of doing this now rather than later.

Aim drives facing and nothing else yet. What a mouse point and a stick direction each mean once there is an ability to place is still open.
